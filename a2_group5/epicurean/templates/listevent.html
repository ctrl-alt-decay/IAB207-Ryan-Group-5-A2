Acknowledgement of Country

What is it? A statement of respect for the Traditional Custodians of the land where an event is held, recognising the continuing connection of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples to Country.

Why it matters Recognising First Peoples and their histories builds the respect and understanding between Indigenous and non-Indigenous Australians that reconciliation depends on.

Not a Welcome to Country Anyone, of any background, can give an Acknowledgement of Country. A Welcome to Country can only be given by Traditional Owners, or Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people they've given permission to, because only Traditional Custodians can welcome visitors onto their Country.

Choose whether your event page includes an Acknowledgement of Country. Enhanced helps you write one that fits your event.

Avoid

Saying "welcome". Only Traditional Owners, or people they've given permission to, give a Welcome to Country.

Vague wording like "the Aboriginal people of this land" when the nation can be named.

Treating maps or postcodes as exact. Boundaries can be contested - if you're unsure, acknowledge "the Traditional Custodians" rather than guess.

Copy-pasting the same words for every event.
